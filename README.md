# CO2 Emissions from the Combustion of Liquid and Gaseous Fuels in Austria

This project is part of the bachelor thesis from Elijah Stängl under the supervision of Johannes Schmidt.

## Abstract

To meet the self-defined target of climate neutrality by 2040, Austria’s greenhouse gas
emissions need to decrease significantly each year. The largest share of emissions is fossil fuel
combustion in the energy sector. Official emission data for Austria is published with a delay
of up to 2 years, which postpones the analysis of emission trends. This bachelor thesis presents
a method to provide up-to-date CO2 emissions from the combustion of liquid and gaseous fuels
and shows the resulting data on an online platform. The emissions are calculated with regularly
updated data for fuel combustion. Various processing steps are necessary to address missing
consumption data and exclude non-energetic uses of fossil fuels. The results differ from official
emission data by substantially less than 2% on average across all categories. The approach
works well to analyse emission trends with a short time lag. Future work may include emissions
from the industry sectors.

## Project

The goal of this project is to calculate up-to-date CO2 emissions from fuel combustion in Austria. The results should mimic official emission data published in Austria's National Inventory Document.

Data from Eurostat, E-Control, Austria's Common Reporting Table 2026 as well as Austria's National Inventory Document 2026 is used.

A complete documentation is provided in the accompanying [bachelor thesis](docs\bachelor_thesis_CO2_in_Austria.pdf).
